How the simulator works

The selected annual portfolio return is treated as an effective annual total-return assumption and converted into an equivalent monthly rate.

Each simulated month credits portfolio growth first and then deducts that month's distribution. If inflation escalation is selected, the monthly distribution increases once each year by the chosen inflation rate.

Option 1 — Maximum Income: the default monthly distribution is set equal to the portfolio's equivalent monthly return on the starting capital. Under the model's constant-return assumptions, this targets preservation of starting capital in nominal ringgit.

Option 2 — Preserve Real Capital: the calculator numerically solves for the highest starting monthly distribution, escalating with inflation, that leaves ending nominal capital approximately equal to the inflation-adjusted value required to preserve the starting capital's purchasing power.

If the assumed portfolio return is too low relative to inflation, a positive inflation-linked distribution may not be compatible with full real-capital preservation. In that case the simulator sets the strategy default to zero rather than implying an unsustainable income level.

Option 3 — Balanced: the default starting distribution equals 2.4% of initial capital annually, paid monthly and increased with inflation. This is an illustrative compromise, not a guaranteed sustainable-withdrawal rule.

Real capital is calculated by discounting the nominal ending balance by cumulative assumed inflation.

MGS / GII implementation note:
A bond's yield to maturity is not the same as its coupon or profit distribution. Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues normally make periodic cash payments rather than generating a smooth monthly total return. Purchase price, accrued interest, coupon/profit rate, maturity value, reinvestment rates and dealing spreads can therefore cause actual cash flows to differ from this simplified model.
Important:
This simulator assumes constant returns and inflation for planning purposes and ignores taxes, fees, bid/offer spreads, liquidity costs, reinvestment differences and changes in market yields. It is a planning tool, not a guarantee of investment results.
Point-in-time reference:
The 4.17% selection is included as a 10-year MGS benchmark reference for 15 September 2026. The 1.8% CPI selection is included as a recent Malaysian CPI reference. These references are not automatically updated.
