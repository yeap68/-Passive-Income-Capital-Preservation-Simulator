6. Assumptions & methodology

Market reference versus simulation assumptions

The market-reference figures provide context for current conditions. The 10-year MGS benchmark yield is used as the initial reference for the simulator's default return assumption.

Latest Malaysia CPI inflation YoY measures the change in consumer prices compared with the same period one year earlier. It is not automatically used as the long-term inflation assumption.

The long-term average inflation assumption represents an assumed compound average over the selected projection horizon. Actual annual inflation will vary.

Custom return and inflation assumptions may also be entered manually for scenario and stress testing.

Maximum Distribution

The starting monthly distribution is calculated from the effective monthly return implied by the selected annual portfolio return.

With distribution escalation switched off, the strategy targets preservation of starting nominal ringgit capital.

Preserve Real Capital

The simulator calculates a starting distribution designed so that ending nominal capital is sufficient to preserve the purchasing power of the starting capital after applying the selected long-term average inflation assumption.

The distribution-treatment checkbox separately determines whether the monthly distribution itself increases annually with inflation.

Balanced

The default starting distribution is 2.4% of initial capital per year, paid monthly. The remaining portfolio return is retained to provide a balance between current distributions and capital growth.

Nominal versus real capital

Nominal capital is the future ringgit balance. Real capital converts that future balance into today's purchasing power using the selected long-term average inflation assumption.

Preserving RM1,000,000 nominally is therefore different from preserving the purchasing power of RM1,000,000 .

Important MGS / GII limitation

This simulator uses a portfolio total-return assumption. Actual Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues generally make semiannual coupon or profit payments.

Yield to maturity is not the same as the cash coupon or profit rate. Purchase price, accrued interest, pull-to-par, reinvestment rates and dealing spreads can therefore make actual cash flows differ from this simplified monthly model.

What is not modelled

The simulator does not model taxes, fees, bid-offer spreads, individual bond maturities, reinvestment timing, credit events, changes in interest rates, sequence-of-return risk or annual fluctuations in inflation.
