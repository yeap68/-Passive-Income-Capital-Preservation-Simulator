6. Assumptions & methodology

How the calculation works

The selected annual portfolio return is treated as an effective annual total return and converted to an equivalent monthly rate. Portfolio growth is credited monthly and the selected distribution is deducted at the end of each month.

Inflation is always applied when calculating the future purchasing power of capital. The inflation assumption therefore affects real capital even when the distribution-escalation option is not selected.

By default, monthly distributions remain constant in nominal ringgit. If “Maintain the purchasing power of my monthly distribution” is selected, the distribution increases once each year by the selected inflation rate.

Maximum Income

The default monthly distribution is the amount generated by the equivalent monthly portfolio return on starting capital. With distribution escalation switched off, this targets preservation of starting nominal capital under the model's constant-return assumptions.

Inflation still reduces the purchasing power of that nominal capital and of a fixed monthly distribution over time.

Preserve Real Capital

The simulator solves for a starting monthly distribution that targets an ending nominal portfolio equal to starting capital compounded by the selected inflation rate over the planning horizon.

This strategy is intended to preserve the purchasing power of the capital itself. This is different from selecting the option to maintain the purchasing power of monthly distributions.

Balanced

The default Balanced distribution starts at 2.4% of initial capital per year, divided into monthly distributions.

The objective is to provide meaningful distributions while retaining a greater portion of expected portfolio return for capital growth.

Nominal versus real capital

Nominal capital is the future ringgit balance. Real capital converts that future balance into today's purchasing power using the selected inflation assumption.

Preserving RM1 million nominally is therefore different from preserving the purchasing power of RM1 million.

Maintaining distribution purchasing power

A fixed nominal monthly distribution buys less over time when prices rise. Selecting “Maintain the purchasing power of my monthly distribution” increases the distribution annually by the selected inflation rate.

This maintains approximately the same real spending power of the distribution, but the larger future withdrawals may reduce or eventually deplete capital more quickly.

Important MGS / GII limitation

This simulator uses a portfolio total-return assumption. Actual Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues generally make semiannual coupon or profit payments.

Yield to maturity is not the same as the cash coupon or profit rate. Purchase price, accrued interest, pull-to-par, reinvestment rates and dealing spreads can therefore make actual cash flows differ from this simplified monthly model.

What is not modelled

The model does not include taxes, transaction costs, bank spreads, custody fees, changes in future interest rates, reinvestment risk, credit events or changes in inflation through time.

Planning tool only. Point-in-time MGS/GII yield and CPI references are not automatically updated market data.
