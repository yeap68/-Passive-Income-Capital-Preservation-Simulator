6. What each strategy means

Option 1 — Maximum Income

This option sets the monthly distribution equal to the modeled equivalent monthly return on the starting capital.

For example, an annual effective return is first converted to an equivalent monthly rate. The portfolio grows by that monthly rate and the distribution is then deducted. Under a constant return and no inflation escalation, this mathematically targets preservation of the starting nominal capital.

This does not preserve purchasing power when inflation is positive. RM5 million ten years from now will generally buy less than RM5 million today.

Option 2 — Preserve Real Capital

This option first calculates how large the portfolio must become at the end of the selected horizon to retain the starting capital's purchasing power.

It then solves for the highest modeled starting monthly distribution, increasing annually with inflation, that still reaches approximately that inflation-adjusted capital target.

If expected return is too low relative to inflation, a positive distribution may be impossible while fully preserving real capital.

Option 3 — Balanced

The default Balanced strategy begins with annual distributions equal to 2.4% of initial capital, or 0.2% per month.

For RM5 million, that is RM10,000 per month initially. The default distribution rises annually with inflation.

This retains more portfolio return than Maximum Income, but it does not automatically guarantee full preservation of real capital.

7. Assumptions & methodology

Monthly-return convention The selected annual return is treated as an effective annual total-return assumption. It is converted to an equivalent monthly rate using (1 + annual return)^(1/12) − 1.
Distribution timing For modeling purposes, investment growth is credited first each month and the monthly distribution is deducted at month-end.
Inflation escalation When enabled, the monthly distribution increases once after each completed year by the selected annual inflation rate.
Real capital Ending real capital equals the future nominal balance divided by the cumulative inflation factor over the selected horizon.
MGS / GII cash-flow reality A government security's yield to maturity is not the same thing as its coupon or profit-payment rate. Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues generally make semiannual payments. Purchase price, accrued interest, premium/discount, maturity value and reinvestment rates all affect actual cash flows.
This simulator uses total-return assumptions It does not imply that a 4.17% MGS yield produces 4.17% of immediately spendable coupon cash every year. If a bond is purchased below par, part of its yield may be realized through pull-to-par at maturity. Conversely, a premium bond can have a coupon above its yield.
Monthly-income implementation Because MGS/GII cash flows are normally semiannual rather than monthly, an actual passive-income portfolio may need a cash reserve, short-term deposit or money-market/Treasury-bill bucket to smooth distributions between coupon dates.
Not included The projection does not model bid/offer spreads, custody costs, taxes, transaction fees, changing reinvestment yields, security-specific duration, defaults, early sales, or future changes in inflation.

The 4.17% 10-year MGS reference and 1.8% CPI reference are point-in-time planning references embedded in this version of the simulator and are not automatically updated.

8. Important investment interpretation

This is a planning calculator, not a promise of future investment performance. Government securities can provide strong nominal capital-protection characteristics when held to maturity, but market values fluctuate before maturity and sovereign, reinvestment, inflation, liquidity and execution risks remain.

For an actual MGS/GII portfolio, calculations should use the specific security's clean price, accrued interest, dirty price, face value, coupon/profit rate, payment dates, maturity date and executable bank quote.
